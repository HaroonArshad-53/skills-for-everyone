# Proposal Writer

## When to use this skill
- Applying to a freelance job post on any platform
- Pitching a client directly by email or DM
- Rewriting a proposal that got no replies

## Workflow
1. Read the job post first. Pull out the client number-one problem, the deliverables, and any specific words, tools, or details only a careful reader would catch.
2. Ask for (or use what the user gave): the user 2-3 most relevant past results, tools they actually know, and one proof item (link, number, or outcome). If proof is missing, write the proposal without inventing any.
3. Open with the client problem and a specific detail from their post - never with Dear Sir/Madam, I am an expert with 5 years experience.
4. Paragraph 2: your plan in 3-4 concrete steps, in the order you would actually do the work.
5. Paragraph 3: proof - the most relevant past result, stated exactly as the user provided it.
6. Close with one low-friction next step: a short call, a small first milestone, or one clear question.
7. Match length to platform: Upwork and direct proposals 150-250 words unless the post asks specific questions - then answer every question first.

## Rules - never break these
- Never invent experience, clients, numbers, ratings, or portfolio links. If the user has no proof yet, use a credible first-job angle: a small paid trial milestone and a relevant sample they do have.
- Never promise a specific result you cannot control (guarantee number-1 ranking, sales in 7 days). Promise process and deliverables instead.
- No buzzword stuffing: avoid synergy, guru, ninja, passionate team player. Specific beats impressive.
- Write in the user own simple voice if they are not a native English speaker - clean and professional, not fake-corporate.

## Output format
- The proposal - ready to paste, in the structure above
- Why this angle - 1 line: which client pain the opening targets
- One thing to personalise before sending - the single detail to double-check

## Example
**User:** Write me an Upwork proposal for this logo design job post. I have done 3 logos for local shops, I use Illustrator, no online reviews yet.

**Good output (excerpt):** Opening line written for that post: Your post mentions the current logo looks fine on the shop sign but unreadable on Instagram - that is a scalability problem, not a design-taste problem, and it is fixable in the first concept round. No experience was invented; the 3 local-shop logos are used as proof.
