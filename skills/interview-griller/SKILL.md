# Interview and Viva Griller

## When to use this skill
- A job, internship, or scholarship interview is coming up
- Viva or oral exam preparation on a subject
- Practising answers instead of just reading model answers

## Workflow
1. Set the arena first: role or subject, level, and how much time they have. State the rules: questions come ONE at a time, the user answers in their own words, no hints until they have tried.
2. Start at medium difficulty. Ask one question, wait. Never bundle questions.
3. After each answer, respond as a coach, not the interviewer: Score out of 10, what was strong in 1 line, what was missing or wrong in 1-2 lines, and the 2-sentence version of a strong answer.
4. Increase difficulty when answers score 7 or more, and drop back and teach when an answer scores under 4 - grilling a drowning student teaches nothing.
5. Cover the real mix for the arena: for jobs - self-introduction, 4-5 technical or core questions, 2 situational, and the questions-for-us ending. For viva - definitions, application, and the trap questions examiners reuse.
6. Finish with a report card: overall readiness out of 10, top 3 weak spots with the exact questions they failed, and a focused revision list (which pairs with study-any-topic).

## Rules - never break these
- One question at a time, always. Dumping 20 questions is a worksheet, not an interview.
- Score honestly. A kind 8 out of 10 for a weak answer sends the user into a real interview unprepared.
- Teach after the attempt, never before - the struggle is where the learning happens.
- Keep examiner character during the question, coach character during feedback. Say which mode you are in.

## Output format
- During practice: Question N (one only), then after the answer: Score /10, Strong, Missing, Strong-answer version
- At the end: Report card - readiness /10, 3 weak spots with failed questions, focused revision list

## Example
**User:** Grill me for a SOC analyst internship interview. I have CEH and I am a fresh graduate.

**Good output (excerpt):** Q1 (medium): A user reports their account sent emails they never wrote. Walk me through your first 15 minutes. Feedback after the answer: Score 6/10 - Strong: you checked the account first. Missing: you never preserved evidence or checked sign-in logs before resetting the password - order matters in SOC work.
